# Mahispal Dynasty

Ancient buffalo-herding dynasty that allegedly ruled Kathmandu Valley for 161 years. One of Nepal's greatest unsolved historical mysteries.

## The Story

According to the Gopalraj Vamshavali, after the Gopal dynasty (cow-herders), the Mahispal dynasty (buffalo-herders) ruled the Kathmandu Valley.

**Three Kings:**
| King | Reign |
|------|-------|
| Barsima (Bharsingh) | 49 years |
| Jayasima (Jayasingh) | 71 years, 2 months |
| Bhuvan Simma | 41 years |
| **Total** | **161 years, 2 months** |

## The Mystery

**Zero archaeological evidence:**
- No coins
- No inscriptions
- No palaces
- No weapons or statues
- No site directly linked to them

The Gopalraj Vamshavali was written ~600 years ago during the Malla period — hundreds or thousands of years after the supposed Mahispal reign.

## Four Theories of Origin

1. **Kol-Munda** — Austro-Asiatic community (D.R. Regmi, Gopal Singh Nepali)
2. **Naga** — Naga-worshipping communities (Inrish Nepal)
3. **Tibeto-Burman** — "Nepal" = "Ne" (cattle) + "Pal" (keeper) (Kamal Prasad Malla)
4. **Abhira** — Pastoral community from Central Asia (Baburam Acharya) — most accepted

## The Verdict

Likely a **real pastoral community** that was later transformed into a dynastic narrative by Malla-period writers to legitimize their rule. Not entirely history, not entirely fiction.

## The Fall

- Last king: Bhuvan Simma
- Defeated by first Kirat king, Yalambar
- No contemporary records of this transition
- May have been gradual migration, not a single battle
