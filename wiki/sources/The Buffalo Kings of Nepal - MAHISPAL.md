# The Buffalo Kings of Nepal: MAHISPAL

Documentary by [[IN-Depth Story]] exploring the mysterious Mahispal Dynasty that allegedly ruled Kathmandu Valley for 161 years.

## Summary

Did a dynasty of buffalo-herding kings really rule Kathmandu Valley for 161 years, or is the Mahispal Vansha merely a legend created centuries later? The video examines Nepal's ancient history through chronicles, mythological texts, historical interpretations, and archaeological evidence.

## The Three Kings

According to the Gopalraj Vamshavali (Nepal's oldest chronicle):

| King | Reign |
|------|-------|
| Shri Barsima (Bharsingh) | 49 years |
| Shri Jayasima (Jayasingh) | 71 years, 2 months |
| Shri Bhuvan Simma | 41 years |
| **Total** | **161 years, 2 months** |

## The Mystery

**No archaeological evidence exists:**
- No coins minted by Mahispal rulers
- No inscriptions mentioning the kings
- No palaces, weapons, or statues found
- No archaeological site directly linked to them

## Four Theories of Origin

### 1. Kol-Munda (Austro-Asiatic)
- Historian D.R. Regmi and anthropologist Gopal Singh Nepali
- Earliest inhabitants of Kathmandu Valley were Kol community
- Cow-herding group became Gopals, buffalo-herding became Mahispals
- Connection to Newar Jyapu community

### 2. Naga Connection
- Presented by Inrish Nepal in Rolamba journal
- Kathmandu Valley was once a lake with Nagas
- Nagas were not mythical serpents but ancient human communities
- Lived in hills around valley: Nagarjun, Phulchoki, Shivapuri

### 3. Tibeto-Burman
- Linguist Kamal Prasad Malla's theory
- "Nepal" = "Ne" (cattle) + "Pal" (keeper) = "keeper of cattle"
- Both Gopals and Mahispals linked to Tibeto-Burman pastoral communities
- Historian Sudarshan Raj Tiwari: connection closer to Mahispals than Gopals

### 4. Abhira (Most Widely Accepted)
- Historian Baburam Acharya, Gyanmani Nepal, Kulchandra Koirala, Modnath Prasit
- Abhiras = ancient pastoral community from Central Asia
- Migrated into Indian subcontinent, then Himalayan regions
- Connected to Yaduvanshi community
- **Problem:** Timeline doesn't match — Abhira dynasty in India is much later than Mahispals

## Key Sources

- **Gopalraj Vamshavali** — Nepal's oldest chronicle, written ~600 years ago during Malla period
- **Nepal Mahatmya** — part of Skanda Purana, composed ~9th century
- **Daniel Wright's History of Nepal** — British historian
- **D.R. Regmi's Ancient Nepal** — compared three different sources

## The Verdict

The Mahispal dynasty is **neither entirely history nor entirely fiction:**

1. Ancient pastoral communities herding cows and buffalo likely existed in Kathmandu Valley
2. Oral traditions and local stories about them survived for generations
3. Malla-period writers gave political form to those old memories
4. The dynastic narrative (names, reign periods, succession) was likely created later to legitimize Malla rule

**The truth lies somewhere in between** — a real community transformed into a dynasty through mythological stories and political needs.

## Why It Was Created

Like many dynasties worldwide, Malla rulers wanted to show:
- Legitimacy of their reign
- Antiquity of their rule
- Connection to sacred traditions
- Continuous royal history from Gopals → Mahispals → Kirats → Licchavis → Mallas

## The Fall

- Last Mahispal king: Bhuvan Simma
- Defeated by first Kirat king, Yalambar
- No contemporary records of this transition
- May have been gradual migration/cultural assimilation, not a single battle
- Or simply: lack of grazing land forced buffalo-herders to move elsewhere
