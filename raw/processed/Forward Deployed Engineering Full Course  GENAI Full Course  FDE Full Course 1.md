FDE Full Course #1  
  
Welcome to the Forward Deployed Engineering Series.  
  
In this first lecture, we start from the absolute basics.  
  
Before jumping into Spring AI, APIs, RAG, Agents, Vector Databases, Tool Calling, or any other GenAI concept, it is important to understand two things:  
  
1\. What exactly does a Forward Deployed Engineer do?  
2\. How did Artificial Intelligence evolve into the Generative AI and LLM systems we use today?  
  
In this video, we first understand the role of a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) using a real-world customer support problem.  
  
We see why a requirement like:  
  
“Build an AI chatbot”  
  
is not actually a complete engineering requirement, and how an FDE works closer to the real business problem to design, integrate, and deploy a reliable solution.  
  
After that, we go through the evolution of AI from first principles:  
  
\* Traditional programming and rule-based systems  
\* Symbolic AI  
\* Early chatbots and expert systems  
\* ELIZA and the illusion of understanding  
\* Machine Learning  
\* Training, models, parameters, and inference  
\* Neural Networks and Deep Learning  
\* Feature Engineering and Representation Learning  
\* Why human language is difficult for computers  
\* Statistical Language Models  
\* N-Grams and next-word prediction  
\* RNNs and sequential processing  
\* Long-range dependency problems  
\* Attention  
\* Transformers  
\* Large Language Models  
\* What GPT actually means: Generative, Pre-trained, Transformer  
  
The goal of this series is not to make GenAI feel tied to one programming language.  
  
The concepts will remain mostly language-independent so that anyone interested in building modern AI applications can follow along.  
  
In the upcoming lectures, we will go deeper into:  
  
Tokens, Embeddings, Context Windows, Prompting, Temperature, LLM APIs, Structured Outputs, RAG, Vector Databases, Tool Calling, Agents, MCP, Evaluation, Guardrails, and production-ready GenAI applications.  
  
If you are a developer trying to understand how modern AI applications are actually engineered not just how to call an LLM APIthis series is for you.
